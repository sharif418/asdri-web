# Brief — the ASDRI home page, done as well as it can be done

You are the lead engineer and design lead on this project for one long, uninterrupted session. The site exists, it is live on staging, and it works. This brief is not about making it work. It is about making the home page of As-Sunnah Dawah & Research Institute the best page of its kind in Bangladesh, and a page that would not embarrass the institute beside the best institutional sites anywhere.

Repository: `https://github.com/sharif418/asdri-web`. Token with read and write access to contents and pull requests on this repository only: `<GITHUB_TOKEN>`. Staging, with the client's starter content loaded: `https://asdri.ailearnersbd.com` (Bangla) and `https://asdri.ailearnersbd.com/en`.

## The client, in one paragraph

As-Sunnah Dawah & Research Institute is the educational institution of As-Sunnah Foundation, the largest Islamic charity in Bangladesh. It takes young ulama from the qawmi madrasas and graduates from the universities and trains them, for months or years, to answer the modern world's intellectual challenges from classical knowledge. Qualified students study, live and eat free, paid for by the Foundation's Zakat Fund. Its people are readers of kitabs: a main text with commentary in the margin, ruled pages, restrained illumination. Admissions are announced on Facebook today. Donors, many abroad, want to know where their Zakat goes. The institute has never had a website.

## What the client is afraid of

They have said it more clearly than anything else about requirements: the site must look like a serious institution made it, and it must never look like a template or an AI-generated page. They cannot tell you what font or spacing is wrong; they will only say it does not feel right. They will compare it with the Foundation's own site, assunnahfoundation.org, which this site must visibly belong to and quietly surpass in typography and reading comfort. Every judgement on your work will be made by that standard, by eye, on a phone, by people who have looked at a great many Islamic institution websites and found almost all of them interchangeable.

## What exists, and why it is not enough

Read `AGENTS.md` and `docs/00` to `docs/09`, then look at staging the way the client will: on a phone first, then on a laptop, in Bangla, then in English. The home page has every section the client asked for in their documents (`docs/source/`, the `.extracted.txt` files are the only source of Bangla copy): the hero with the institute's name and an admission slip in the margin, the impact figures, the vision and its pillars, the featured programmes, the latest notices, campus life, the people, the support band, a three-row masthead with dropdown menus, and a footer. The design system behind it is in `docs/05` and `docs/08`, with a specimen page at `/design`: serif Bangla for reading, sans for interface, green for action, one gold line per screen, structure drawn with rules rather than cards, the matn-and-hashiya reading layout.

It is correct, and it is a skeleton. It has the bones of a distinguished page and none of the confidence. Spacing is uniform where it should breathe and tighten with meaning. Sections sit one after another without a sense of sequence, as if the page did not know what it wanted the reader to feel first and do next. The masthead is sensible and forgettable. The menu is a list. The empty states are honest but flat. Nothing on the page would make a visitor from Dhaka or Birmingham or Riyadh stop and think: these people are serious, and they care about what they make. That is the gap you are here to close.

## What we want from you

Make this home page, and the navigation that frames it, extraordinary. Not different for its own sake: deeper. Take everything you know about typography, rhythm, hierarchy, Bangla type on screens, how a reader's eye moves through a long page on a phone, how an institution of learning presents itself without shouting, and spend all of it here. Look hard at the best institutional and scholarly sites in the world before you touch a line, not to borrow their look but to calibrate your sense of what finished feels like, then come back to this client's material culture and make something that could only be theirs.

The client's documents are your content and your brief for what the page must contain and in what order; the design documents are the foundation you build on. Within that, you have real freedom. If the type scale needs another step, add it. If the masthead wants to become something a visitor remembers, make it so. If a section the client listed reads better as two, or two read better as one, do it and say why. If the menu should reveal the institute's structure rather than hide it, design that. If the page needs a device the design system does not yet have, design the device, add it to `/design` so it belongs to the system, and use it. The design system should be stronger when you are done, not bypassed. Treat the tokens, components and documents as yours to improve, and improve them in a way the next engineer can follow.

Build with the real content, in both languages, and let the Bangla lead. Bengali digits where the system says so. Every string a staff member might change stays in Payload; every interface string stays in the dictionaries; every page works with the least data an office might have entered and looks intentional in that state. Keep the stack, the locale routing, the infrastructure and the decisions in `docs/adr/`; everything else is open to your judgement, and where this brief and the documents are silent, choose the better option and record it in the pull request.

Work on one branch from a fresh `main`, push as soon as something coherent exists and again before you stop, and open a single pull request. Do not merge it. In the pull request, write what you saw that was wrong, what you changed and why, and what you would do next with another day. Capture the home page and the open menu at phone width and at desktop width in both languages under `docs/review/home-v2/`, and look at your own screenshots as the client would before you commit them. If something in them would make you hesitate to show it to the client, it is not done.

We will review the result on staging and in the screenshots. If it is better, it ships. If it is not, we roll back with git and nothing is lost. So do not hedge. This is the page the institute will be judged by, and it is the page you will be judged by. Make it the one people in this country point to when they want to show what a dawah institute's website can be.
